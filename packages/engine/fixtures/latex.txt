Show that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, $\sum_{k=1}^{n} k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.

Recall the Gaussian integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\,dx = \sqrt{\pi}$,
and the identity $\left\| A \right\|_F^2 = \operatorname{tr}(A^\top A)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) &= -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log p_\theta(y_i \mid x_i) \\
  \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} &= -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(y_i \mid x_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

Inline edge cases: \\ (a literal double backslash), \{braces\}, 100\% of cases,
a\_b subscript escape, and $x^{2^{n}}$ with nested superscripts.
